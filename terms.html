1. What Grail does

Grail identifies baseball cards from a photograph, estimates their condition, shows a value range based on recent market sales, and keeps a record of the cards you save.

2. Valuations are estimates, not appraisals

This is the most important term in this document. Every value Grail shows is an estimate. It is not an appraisal, not a guarantee, and not an offer to buy or sell. Do not treat it as financial or investment advice.

  • Value ranges are derived from recent completed sales of cards our providers believe match yours. What your particular card actually sells for may be higher or lower, and often will be.
  • Condition strongly affects price, and Grail judges condition from a single photo of the front. It cannot see the back, and one photo hides most surface defects. A professional grader will reach their own conclusion, and it may differ substantially.
  • Where recent sales data is unavailable, Grail may show an estimate produced by an AI model instead. The app labels these differently, and they are less reliable.
  • Card markets move. A value shown today may be wrong tomorrow, and values stored in your collection reflect the moment the card was added.

You are solely responsible for any decision you make to buy, sell, insure, grade or hold a card. We are not liable for any loss arising from a valuation, an identification, or a condition estimate shown in the app.

3. Identification may be wrong

Card recognition is automated and imperfect, particularly for parallels, variants, reprints and poorly lit photographs. Grail shows a match confidence, and offers a way to flag an incorrect result. Always verify a card yourself before acting on the result.
